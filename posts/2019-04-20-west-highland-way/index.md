*165 km from the outskirts of Glasgow to the foot of Ben Nevis, with a tent on our backs.*

The [West Highland Way](https://www.westhighlandway.org/) is Scotland's oldest long distance trail: from Milngavie, just north of Glasgow, along the whole length of Loch Lomond, over Rannoch Moor and through Glen Coe to Fort William. Over Easter 2019 my very good friend Marti and I walked it in seven days, carrying everything we needed and sleeping mostly in the tent.

We were lucky with the weather. The first days were the famous Easter heatwave: sun, blue sky and Loch Lomond like a mirror. Only from Rannoch Moor on did Scotland remember what it's supposed to look like.

## The story

### Day 1: Milngavie to Drymen, 22 km

After breakfast in Glasgow we took the obligatory photos in Milngavie under the **start arch** and by the granite obelisk. The first day is gentle: Mugdock woods, fields, lambs everywhere, and the first **Highland cows** of the trip, so of course we stopped for those. Lunch was soup and a burger at a place with **chinchillas**.

The rest of the afternoon was farmland and sheep, all the way to Drymen. We pitched the tent just outside it and the day ended by a **campfire**.

### Day 2: Drymen to Loch Lomond, 29 km

The first real hill: **Conic Hill**, right on the Highland Boundary Fault. The view from it over **Loch Lomond and its islands** is one of the best of the whole way, even in the morning haze. Down in Balmaha we had lunch in the sun and said hello to the statue of **Tom Weir**.

From there the path follows the shore of the loch through the woods. We walked until the evening, past Rowardennan, and slept by the water with the loch going quiet and blue.

### Day 3: Loch Lomond to Inverarnan, 21 km

Waking up to **Loch Lomond like a mirror** and cooking breakfast on the pebble beach was one of the highlights of the trip.

The northern half of the loch is the roughest bit of the whole way: roots, rocks, ups and downs through the woods. We peeked into the **Rowchoish bothy**, had coffee at the Inversnaid Hotel with a view of the loch, and looked at the **Inversnaid waterfall**. In the afternoon we finally left the loch behind, looked back down it one last time and walked into the campsite at **Beinglas Farm** in Inverarnan.

### Day 4: Inverarnan to Bridge of Orchy, 31 km

The longest day. Up Glen Falloch along the river and under the railway, then over to Strathfillan with the views opening up. Past the **Lochan of the Lost Sword** to Tyndrum, where a big lunch and a sign saying **"West Highland Way 68 km to go"** were waiting.

The evening walk to Bridge of Orchy was magical: the perfect cone of **Beinn Dòrain** in front of us, gorse in bloom and **Highland cows** grazing below it in the last sun. We camped by the river at Bridge of Orchy, and in the evening wrote postcards home.

### Day 5: Bridge of Orchy to Altnafeadh, 25 km

In the morning over the hill above **Loch Tulla**, where the weather finally turned Scottish: wind, grey sky and the red hat on. Then **Rannoch Moor**: a long old road through brown, empty, beautiful nothing.

We warmed up with a curry at the **Glencoe Mountain** café, and at **Kingshouse** the **stags** walk right between the cars and the tents. We pitched the tent near Altnafeadh, right below the **Buachaille Etive Mòr**. Hands down the most spectacular place on the whole route.

### Day 6: Altnafeadh to Kinlochleven, 11 km

The shortest day, so we added a bonus. After the **Devil's Staircase**, the highest point of the way, we went up **Stob Mhic Mhartuin** next to it for a view over the Mamores and Glen Coe.

Then down to **Kinlochleven** along the huge pipes of the old aluminium smelter. The smelter now houses the **Ice Factor**, an indoor climbing and ice climbing centre, so we had a look inside.

### Day 7: Kinlochleven to Fort William, 25 km

Back up out of Kinlochleven and into the **Lairig Mòr**, a long empty valley on an old military road past ruined houses.

In the middle of the Lairig Mòr we met two Scottish hikers. One of them had a finger in a cast, so I asked what had happened. That morning he had slipped and broken it, and his buddy took him to the hospital. Surprised, I asked what he was doing here, in the middle of the moors. He said he had about two hours before the anaesthetic wore off, and he wanted to finish the route. Scottish people are built different.

Then through the forest above **Glen Nevis**, with Ben Nevis hiding in the clouds.

In Fort William we ended at the **sore feet statue**, which is exactly how we felt. A proper dinner and a proper bed followed.

## Learnings

- [+] **Seven days**
  Long enough to enjoy it, with time for coffee, bothies and a side summit.
- [+] **The tent**
  No need to plan the nights: [wild camping is allowed in Scotland](https://www.outdooraccess-scotland.scot/) for a small tent, two or three nights in one place, away from roads and houses, leaving no trace. Only on the east shore of Loch Lomond you need a [permit](https://www.lochlomond-trossachs.org/things-to-do/camping/get-a-permit/) from March to September.
- [+] **Camping below the Buachaille Etive Mòr**
  Hands down the most spectacular place on the whole route. Improvise the other nights, but this one is a must stop.
- [+] **Spring**
  No midges, lambs everywhere, and with a bit of luck the sun.
