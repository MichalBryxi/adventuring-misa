I've had a few epics and some genuinely bad days out there, and these rules came from them. Mountains aren't a playground, and nobody's mum is coming to pick you up when it gets too scary.

1. **Microspikes. Always.** They weigh nothing, and when you need them, nothing else will do.
2. **An ice axe.** I still leave it at home too often. On steep snow, and on steep grass, it's the difference between a harmless slip and a fall.
3. **A few metres of accessory cord.** Enough to improvise a harness, use a boulder as an anchor, or leave a bit of tat in a nasty spot for whoever comes next.
4. **A way to call for help.** I own a [Garmin inReach](https://www.garmin.com/en-US/c/outdoor-recreation/satellite-communicators/) and take it on bigger, multi-day trips. For everything else, I rely on my phone's [satellite SOS](https://support.apple.com/en-us/101573).
5. **A plan someone else knows.** "I should have signal by 3 pm. If you haven't heard from me, check on me."
6. **Dry, stable weather.** If it rained the day before, or today's forecast is shaky, I don't go.
