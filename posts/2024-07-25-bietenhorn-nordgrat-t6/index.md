*A T6 that barely feels like one.*

## The story

### The first attempt

On 15 July 2024 I went with a couple of my friends. We took the **cable car** from Lauterbrunnen up to Grütschalp, which takes care of a good chunk of the altitude, and set off towards the ridge.

Climbing onto the top of the ridge through a **chimney-like gully**, it looked like an **irreversible step** was coming. At the same time we were racing a **cold front**, and there were **snow patches** on the top. Not worth the commitment, so we turned around somewhere around the crux, I think. **The correct call.** More adventures some other time.

### Back alone

Ten days later I came back alone, same cable car, same start.

Not far from Grütschalp there's an old army structure, then a tiny little scramble up the chimney onto the top of the ridge. The move we had bailed at last time turned out to be **reversible** after all. From there it's just following the ridge, with a T5 geocache along the way. Nothing complicated. Wyssbirg is an out-and-back from the ridge.

The weirdest part is the **scree field** under the serac. I was really not sure where it was going, or whether this was even the right way. The line led directly under a **lone hanging serac**, so I **went around it** instead, on less than ideal ground.

Past that I reached Bietenhorn in the clouds, came down to Bietenlücke, had a beer at the Schilthornhütte and walked up to Birg.

On the way up to Birg I passed a long wall of white sheets. I wasn't sure what I was looking at, but it was quite startling: a massive amount of snow, taller than me. Only later did I learn it's the snow from **last season's ski slope**, covered so that it doesn't melt over the summer and the ski season can start earlier.

From Birg I took the gondola down. Unlike most T6s, there's a **gondola at both ends**, so the whole thing is a fairly easygoing day out.

Honestly, I'm not sure I'd call it a T6.

## Learnings

- [+] **Bailed on the first attempt**
  An irreversible step ahead, a cold front coming in and snow patches on the top. We turned back, which was the correct call.
- [+] **Went around the serac**
  The line led straight under a lone hanging serac. I took worse ground instead of risking it falling on my head.
